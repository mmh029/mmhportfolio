02 / Research

Research

01

Bucknell & Geisinger · 2025–2026

Electrochemical sensing &
applied deep learning

Working with Dr. Alan Marchiori on a project evaluating screen-printed electrodes for low-cost calcium sensing. My contribution focused on preparing solutions, collecting electrochemical measurements, and assessing sensitivity and reproducibility.

What the work taught me

The broader project connects chemical analysis with applied deep learning. Reliable experimental data comes first: inconsistent readings led us to troubleshoot the measurement setup and identify a faulty cable between the electrode and potentiostat.

Replacing the cable improved consistency, but the results still needed further testing. This experience taught me to question the entire measurement chain and to treat inconclusive results as information.

02

Semiconductor modeling · COMSOL Multiphysics

GaAs vs. silicon
in gate-all-around FETs

Contributed to a team study comparing GaAs and silicon nanowire channels in gate-all-around field-effect transistors. We used computational modeling to explore how material properties affect behavior at small device scales.

About the study

Our team included Josh Le Quang, Darwin Juca, Cyrus Kuhn, Nathaniel Saltsgiver, and Amal Kabalan. The work involved COMSOL’s semiconductor module and a comparison of transport behavior across the modeled devices.

It gave me experience connecting semiconductor physics with simulation results and communicating technical work through a research poster.
